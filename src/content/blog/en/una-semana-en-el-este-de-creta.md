Agios Nikolaos is well placed for getting to know eastern Crete without changing accommodation. Spinalonga, Kritsa, the Lasithi Plateau and Knossos are an hour's drive away or less, and the farthest spot, Sitia and the Vai palm grove, makes for one long day. This seven-day plan alternates excursions with quieter days, so you don't spend the whole week inside the car.

## Before You Start: Car, Bus and Boat

You don't need a car every day. Regular bus lines run from the Agios Nikolaos bus station to Elounda, Kritsa, Heraklion, Ierapetra and Sitia, and you reach Spinalonga by boat. For the Lasithi Plateau, Mochlos or the full Sitia and Vai route in one day, though, a car is the practical choice: either there's no bus, or there are very few departures.

If you're renting one for just a few days, concentrate them on the days that need it. We sum it up below and, if you'd rather do without one, you'll find more ideas in [how to get around without a car](/blog/moverse-sin-coche/).

## Day 1: Agios Nikolaos on Foot

The first day is unhurried and car-free. From the pension, Kitroplatia beach is a one-minute walk, the harbour is three (with the statue of the Rape of Europa) and Lake Voulismeni is about six. The centre is nine minutes away, so everything can be covered on foot.

- In the morning: [Lake Voulismeni](/blog/lago-voulismeni/) and the streets of the centre.
- In the afternoon: a swim at Kitroplatia and a stroll around the harbour.
- For more ideas, see our guide to [what to see in Agios Nikolaos](/blog/que-ver-en-agios-nikolaos/).

If you get up early, from the pension's balconies you can watch the sun rise over Mirabello Bay.

## Day 2: Spinalonga, Elounda and Plaka

Elounda is about 23 minutes away by car (15 km). Spinalonga, the island with the 16th-century Venetian fortress that was a leper colony until 1957, is reached by boat. From Plaka the crossing is very short, about ten minutes; boats also leave from Elounda, and there are excursions from the harbour in Agios Nikolaos.

This is a day you can do without a car, combining bus and boat. There is little shade on the island: bring water and a hat, and in summer try to go early. More details in [the trip to Spinalonga](/blog/excursion-a-spinalonga/) and in [Elounda and Plaka](/blog/elounda-y-plaka/).

## Day 3: Knossos and the Heraklion Archaeological Museum

Knossos is an hour away by car (65 km). The palace is about 5 km from the centre of Heraklion, where the Archaeological Museum is, so it makes sense to see both on the same day: the site first thing, before the heat and the tour groups, and the museum afterwards.

![Red and black painted columns and stone steps of an ancient palace in low morning light](../../../assets/blog/gen/una-semana-en-el-este-de-creta-2.jpg)

You can go by car, or by bus to Heraklion and then by city bus to Knossos. If you arrive or leave through Heraklion airport (50 minutes away, 62 km), you can move this visit to that day and free up another. We explain it in [how to get here from the airport](/blog/como-llegar-desde-el-aeropuerto-de-heraclion/) and in our article on the [Palace of Knossos](/blog/palacio-de-knossos/).

## Day 4: Kritsa and Lato, and an Afternoon at the Beach

Half a day of excursion and half a day of rest. Kritsa is about 11 km away, on the mountainside. Before you reach the village is Panagia Kera, a small Byzantine church with frescoes, and about 3 km farther on are the ruins of Lato, an ancient Dorian city with views over the gulf.

- A bus goes to Kritsa; for Lato a car is more convenient.
- Check the opening hours of the church and the site beforehand, because they change with the season.
- Come back to Agios Nikolaos for lunch and spend the afternoon at the beach.

There is more in [Kritsa and the surrounding area](/blog/kritsa-y-alrededores/).

## Day 5: Sitia and the Vai Palm Grove

The longest day of the week, and it's worth setting out early. It's a little over an hour by car to Sitia, and nearly two to Vai, at the eastern tip of the island. Along the way you'll find the harbour and museum of Sitia and Toplou Monastery.

To do it all in one day you need a car. We tell you how to organise it in [Sitia and the Vai palm grove](/blog/sitia-y-palmeral-de-vai/).

## Day 6: Rest or Mochlos

After the Vai day, a quiet one. There are beaches for every taste without taking the car: Ámmos about 10 minutes on foot, Ammoudi 18 and Almiros about 26. We go over them in [the beaches of Agios Nikolaos](/blog/playas-de-agios-nikolaos/).

If you feel like getting out for a bit, in the afternoon you can head to [Mochlos](/blog/mochlos/), a fishing village about 45 minutes away by car, on the other side of the gulf, with an islet holding Minoan remains facing the tavernas. It's a good place for fish by the water.

## Day 7: The Lasithi Plateau and the Cave of Zeus

The Cave of Zeus, on the Lasithi Plateau, is an hour away by car (45 km). The plateau is a farming plain surrounded by mountains, at about 850 m above sea level, known for its old windmills. The Dikteon Cave, next to the village of Psychró, is where, according to the myth, Zeus was born.

- You climb to the cave on foot along a short but steep path, and inside there are steps that can be slippery: wear comfortable shoes.
- It's cooler up there than on the coast; outside summer, bring something warm.
- Buses to the plateau are very scarce, so you'll need a car for this day.

![Old windmill with white cloth sails on a green mountain plateau with fields and bare peaks around](../../../assets/blog/gen/una-semana-en-el-este-de-creta-3.jpg)

More ideas in [the Lasithi Plateau](/blog/meseta-de-lasithi/).

## Ierapetra and Chrissi: The Swap for Summer Days

If you're travelling in swimming season, you can swap day 4 or day 6 for Ierapetra and Chrissi Island. Ierapetra, on the south coast, is about 42 minutes away by car (36 km) and also has a bus from Agios Nikolaos. Boats to Chrissi leave from its harbour: an uninhabited, protected island with pale sandy beaches and a juniper forest.

- The boats run in season, roughly from May to October, and may be cancelled in strong wind.
- Depending on the boat, the crossing takes from half an hour to a little over an hour.
- The rules for visiting the island have become stricter in recent years: confirm the conditions when you book.
- There is little shade and few facilities: bring water and sun protection.

## Which Days You Need a Car

- **No car:** day 1 (Agios Nikolaos) and day 2 (Spinalonga, by bus and boat).
- **Car or bus:** day 3 (Knossos and Heraklion), day 4 (Kritsa; Lato is better by car) and Ierapetra.
- **Car:** day 5 (Sitia and Vai), day 7 (Lasithi) and Mochlos.

## Making the Week Work

- Don't schedule two long driving days back to back; that's why the rest day comes after Vai.
- In July and August, visit sites and museums in the morning and leave midday for the beach or the shade.
- Opening hours for museums, sites and boats change with the season. Check them on their official websites or on the spot before setting out.
- If you get a day of bad weather, see [what to do when it rains](/blog/que-hacer-cuando-llueve/) and swap the order of the days: the plan is flexible.
- If you're still deciding on dates, see our guide to [when to visit Agios Nikolaos](/blog/cuando-visitar-agios-nikolaos/).

You can reorder the days depending on the weather and the boat, but it's worth keeping two things: a quiet day after each long one, and outdoor visits first thing in the morning. You'll find the distances from the pension to each of these places on [the map on our website](/#location).

The traditional Cretan breakfast was simple: bread or paximadi, olive oil, cheese, olives, a little fruit and, if there was any, honey. Today in Agios Nikolaos you can find everything from that age-old breakfast to an iced coffee to take away on the way to the beach. Here's what's worth trying and where to look for it.

## Greek yoghurt with thyme honey

It's the classic. Strained Greek yoghurt is thick and creamy, and sheep's yoghurt has a more intense flavour than cow's. In Crete it comes with honey, often thyme honey (θυμαρίσιο, *thymarísio*), very aromatic, and sometimes with walnuts.

Another traditional way of having it is with a *glykó tou koutalioú*, the spoon sweets: fruit preserved in syrup that in Greek homes is offered to visitors.

## Bougatsa, sweet or with cheese

Bougatsa is very thin filo pastry with a filling, baked and cut into small pieces. In Crete you'll mostly see two versions:

- **With mizithra:** the version associated with Chania, filled with this fresh cheese and sprinkled with sugar. The contrast between the cheese and the sugar is its charm.
- **With custard:** filled with a semolina custard, with icing sugar and cinnamon on top.

![Freshly baked filo pastry cut into squares, dusted with icing sugar and cinnamon, custard inside](../../../assets/blog/gen/desayunar-en-creta-2.jpg)

Normally you can choose: ask for *me kréma* (with custard) or *me mizíthra* (with mizithra). It's eaten hot, freshly cut.

## Pites: pies to take away

In bakeries it's easy to find pies made with filo or homemade dough, perfect for breakfast on the go:

- **Tiropita (τυρόπιτα):** cheese.
- **Spanakopita (σπανακόπιτα):** spinach and other greens.
- **Kalitsounia (καλιτσούνια):** little fresh-cheese pastries, sweet or savoury depending on the bakery.

The *sfakianes pites*, thin flatbreads filled with mizithra and served with honey, are also a very Cretan breakfast. In [our article on what to eat in Crete](/blog/que-comer-en-creta/) we explain these and other dishes of the island.

## Paximadia, oil and tomato

Paximadi is a bread, often barley, baked twice until dry and hard. It was made this way so that it would keep for a long time, and it's still part of the Cretan table. It is softened with a few drops of water and eaten with olive oil, grated tomato, cheese and oregano: what the tavernas call dakos also works very well in the morning.

If you're interested in the oil, which is at every meal here, there's [our article on Cretan olive oil](/blog/aceite-de-oliva-de-creta/).

In bakeries you'll also see *koulouri*, a ring of sesame bread, very typical across Greece for breakfast on the move.

## For a heartier breakfast

Some cafés and tavernas that open early offer more substantial dishes, especially for a late breakfast:

- **Fried eggs with staka**, the cooked cream of sheep's or goat's milk, very Cretan.
- **Graviera**, the island's mature cheese, sometimes with honey.
- **Apaki**, pork marinated in vinegar and smoked with herbs.
- **Omelettes** with seasonal vegetables and cheese.

They're not everywhere, but if you see them on the menu, they're a good way to get to know the flavours of the island.

## Seasonal fruit

Fruit changes with the calendar. In summer watermelon, melon and grapes abound, and towards the end of summer the figs arrive. Winter is the season of oranges and mandarins.

As in many Greek towns, Agios Nikolaos has a weekly open-air market (*laikí*), with fruit, vegetables, honey and other local products. The day and place can change, so ask in town before you go.

## Coffee, the Greek way

Coffee is almost a ritual. When you order it you'll be asked about sugar, because it's added while it's being made:

- **Skéto:** no sugar.
- **Métrio:** medium sugar, the in-between option.
- **Glykó:** sweet.

![Small copper briki next to a cup of Greek coffee with foam and a tall glass of iced coffee on a café table](../../../assets/blog/gen/desayunar-en-creta-3.jpg)

And these are the most common options:

- **Greek coffee (ellinikós kafés):** made in a small pot, the *briki*, and served unfiltered. Let it settle for a moment and don't drain the last sip, which is the grounds.
- **Frappé:** instant coffee whisked with water and ice until foamy. It was born in Thessaloniki in 1957 and is still a classic.
- **Freddo espresso and freddo cappuccino:** espresso whisked with ice; the second is topped with cold milk foam. Today they're among the most ordered coffees in summer.

If you prefer something caffeine-free, try *malotira*, the Cretan mountain tea (*Sideritis syriaca*), a herbal infusion drunk on its own or with a little honey.

## Where to have breakfast in Agios Nikolaos

- **Bakeries (φούρνος, *foúrnos*):** they open early and are the best place for bougatsa, pites and koulouri to take away.
- **Pastry shops (ζαχαροπλαστείο, *zacharoplastío*):** for sweets and pastries.
- **Cafés by the water:** at Kitroplatia, in the harbour and around Lake Voulismeni there are cafés where you can sit with a view. From the pension, Kitroplatia is a one-minute walk, the harbour three and the lake six.

From the pension's balconies you can watch the sun rise over Mirabello Bay. Afterwards, you only have to walk a few steps down to have breakfast by the sea and, if you feel like it, take your first swim at [one of the nearby beaches](/blog/playas-de-agios-nikolaos/).

## To start the day well

There's no single way to have breakfast in Crete: a coffee with a tiropita in your hand is worth as much as a table with yoghurt, honey and fruit. Hours and what's on offer at bakeries and cafés change with the season, especially outside summer, so it's wise to ask or look online before you head out.

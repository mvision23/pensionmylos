In Crete, large hotels and holiday apartments live side by side with an older, simpler way of staying: the family-run pension. If you have never stayed in one, it is natural not to know quite what to expect. We tell you what they are usually like, how they differ from a hotel and what to ask before booking. We do it from the inside, because we have run one since the 1980s.

## What a pension is in Greece

In Greece, a pension (in Greek, *pansion*) is usually a small place to stay, with few rooms and run by a family. It is often a building of just a few floors, with simple rooms and, in coastal areas, balconies or terraces from which you can see the sea.

![Narrow, quiet lane in a seaside neighbourhood with white houses, blue shutters, geraniums in painted tins, bougainvillea](../../../assets/blog/gen/alojarse-en-una-pension-en-creta-1.jpg)

That said, no two are alike. Some pensions serve breakfast and others do not; some have a reception desk for a good part of the day and in others the family welcomes you when you arrive. So rather than taking anything for granted, it is best to ask.

## How it differs from a hotel

### The personal touch

In a family-run pension you usually deal directly with the people who run it. That has a practical advantage: you can ask about a beach, a taverna or a bus timetable and the answer will come from someone who lives there all year round.

![Corner of a traditional village cafe under a plane tree, with small cups of Greek coffee and two older men chatting](../../../assets/blog/gen/alojarse-en-una-pension-en-creta-2.jpg)

### Reception and hours

Many pensions do not have a reception open 24 hours. It is not usually a problem, but it means you need to let them know your arrival time, especially if your flight lands late or you are coming a long way by road.

### Services

A large hotel usually offers a restaurant, a swimming pool, entertainment and other services. A pension focuses on the basics: a clean room, a good bed and a peaceful place from which to head out and get to know the area. If a particular service matters to you, ask beforehand rather than assuming.

### Atmosphere

With few rooms, the atmosphere is quieter and more personal. The flip side is that noise carries more, so it is worth bearing in mind early in the morning and at night, especially on balconies and in corridors.

## What to ask before booking

These questions avoid most surprises, in a pension or in any small accommodation:

- **Type of bed.** A *twin* room has two single beds; a double has one large bed. If you are travelling as a couple and have a preference, say so when you book.
- **Views.** "Sea view" can mean different things: a front, open view, a side view or a slice of sea between buildings. Ask what exactly can be seen from the room you are booking.
- **Stairs and accessibility.** Many small buildings in Greece do not have a lift. If you are travelling with heavy suitcases, with small children or with mobility problems, ask which floor the room is on and how many steps there are.
- **Arrival time.** Let them know what time you expect to arrive and ask how the key handover works if you arrive late.
- **How to get there.** Ask for the exact address, where a taxi can drop you off and where to park if you are coming by car. If you are coming from the airport, our guide on [how to get here from Heraklion airport](/blog/como-llegar-desde-el-aeropuerto-de-heraclion/) may help.
- **What the price includes.** Breakfast, cleaning, towels, ways to pay. And bear in mind that in Greece there is a per-night tax that is paid at the accommodation and is not always included in the price when you book.

## Some useful customs

- **Toilet paper.** In many places to stay in Greece you are asked not to flush it, because the pipes are narrow; there is a bin next to the toilet for that purpose. If you see a notice, heed it.
- **Breakfast.** If your accommodation does not include it, having breakfast out is part of the trip. We give you ideas in [breakfast in Crete](/blog/desayunar-en-creta/).
- **The season.** In spring and autumn there are fewer people and small pensions are especially peaceful; in July and August it is wise to book further ahead. If you have not yet decided on dates, take a look at [when to visit Agios Nikolaos](/blog/cuando-visitar-agios-nikolaos/).
- **Ask.** The people who run a pension know the area better than any guidebook: what time to go to a beach, how to combine bus and boat, or where to watch the sunset.

## To finish

A pension is not a small hotel: it is another way of staying, simpler and more personal. It works best when you know what to expect, so before booking, clarify the type of bed, the views, the stairs and the arrival time.

Mochlos is a small fishing village on the eastern shore of Mirabello Bay, on the far side of the bay you can see from Agios Nikolaos. It has a small harbour, a handful of streets, tavernas facing the sea and, about 150 metres offshore, an islet with Minoan remains.

It's not a place for doing a thousand things. You come to stroll, swim off the rocks and have lunch or dinner by the water. For that, it's one of the getaways we most like to recommend.

## Getting there from Agios Nikolaos

Mochlos is about 36-38 km away by road, roughly 45 minutes by car. Follow the national road towards Sitia, passing Pachia Ammos and Kavousi, and near the village of Sfaka the turn-off to the coast appears. From there it's about 6 km of paved road, narrow and winding, that drops down to the village with views over the bay.

- Public transport to the village is very limited, so the practical way to go is by car.
- The village is small and the streets by the water are narrow. The easiest thing is to park as soon as you see a space and carry on on foot.
- If you stay for dinner, the drive back will be after dark on that same winding road: bear that in mind when deciding who drives.

## The islet and its Minoan history

The islet you see opposite the village is also called Mochlos. Today it is uninhabited, but in Minoan times it was probably joined to the mainland and served as a harbour, according to the archaeologists who study it.

![Low rocky islet covered in dry scrub, with faint ancient stone foundations, across a calm turquoise channel](../../../assets/blog/gen/mochlos-2.jpg)

The first excavations, in 1908, brought to light a Bronze Age Minoan necropolis on the western part of the islet. The gold jewellery found in its tombs is now displayed in the Heraklion Archaeological Museum. Since 1989, a Greek-American project has continued the excavations and has documented a Minoan town, remains from the Hellenistic period and, on the mainland behind the present-day village, Minoan artisans' workshops.

### Visiting the islet

- The remains are in the open air, with no facilities and hardly any shade. Bring water, a hat and shoes that grip well, because the ground is uneven.
- The walls are ancient and some are unstable: don't climb on them.
- To get there, confident swimmers can swim across on days when the sea is calm. Watch out for boats passing through the channel.
- In season, you can sometimes cross in a small boat from the village; ask at the harbour.

## Swimming in Mochlos

In the village itself there is a small beach of sand and pebbles, with no sunbeds and no lifeguard. Many people prefer to swim from the rocks, where the water gets deep quickly and is very clear. With a mask and snorkel you can see fish and rocky seabed.

- There are sea urchins on the rocks: water shoes are very useful. You'll find more ideas in [what to bring to the beach in Crete](/blog/que-llevar-a-la-playa-en-creta/).
- The coast faces north and, with the north wind, common in summer, the sea gets choppy. Calm days are the best for swimming and for crossing to the islet.
- In the surrounding area there are small coves of rock and pebbles, such as those at Loutres, good for snorkelling.

## Eating by the sea

The reason many people come back to Mochlos is the tavernas along the waterfront, with tables almost in the water and the islet right opposite. The food is that of the Cretan coast: fish of the day, octopus, salads, seasonal vegetables and dishes to share.

![Table by the sea with grilled fish, grilled octopus, a Greek salad, bread and a small carafe of wine](../../../assets/blog/gen/mochlos-3.jpg)

A few simple tips:

- **Ask which fish came in today.** In Greece fresh fish is usually charged by weight, so it pays to see the fish and know what it weighs before you order.
- **Share several small plates.** It's the usual way to eat in a taverna and lets you try more things.
- **Pick your time.** A late lunch after your swim, or an early dinner with the last light on the islet, are good moments.
- **Out of season**, the village is very quiet and not every place opens. If you go in winter, confirm it beforehand.

We don't recommend specific places because they change from one year to the next. What we can give you are pointers for choosing well in our guide on [how to choose a taverna](/blog/como-elegir-una-taberna/).

## Planning the getaway

Mochlos fits well into a long half-day or an unhurried full day. Two ways to plan it:

1. **Afternoon and dinner.** A relaxed morning in Agios Nikolaos, set off after midday, swim off the rocks, stroll around the village and have dinner by the water.
2. **Full day.** Set off in the morning, visit the islet before the heat builds, have lunch at the harbour and spend the afternoon swimming at a nearby cove.

If you want to continue east, Sitia is about 35 km further on. You can combine the two or, if the day is going to be a long one, leave Sitia for another day, following the route in [Sitia and the Vai palm grove](/blog/sitia-y-palmeral-de-vai/).

## Before you go

Bring a swimsuit, water shoes, water and some proper footwear for the islet. Check the wind for the day: if it's blowing hard from the north, it may be better to leave the swim at Mochlos for another time and stick to the stroll and the meal. And if you go in the low season, confirm that somewhere is open for lunch before you make the 45-minute drive.

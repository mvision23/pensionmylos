Travelling solo has one clear advantage: the day is yours. Agios Nikolaos suits it well because it is a small town, easy to explore on foot and well connected by bus and boat with the rest of eastern Crete. Here are a few ideas to help you plan.

## A town to explore on foot

From the pension, in the Mylos seafront area, almost everything is a short stroll away:

- Kitroplatia beach: 1 minute (100 m)
- Harbour, with the statue of the abduction of Europa: 3 minutes (200 m)
- Lake Voulismeni: 6 minutes (450 m)
- Town centre: 9 minutes (600 m)
- Ámmos beach: 10 minutes (650 m)
- Ammoudi beach: 18 minutes (1.2 km)
- Almiros beach: 26 minutes (2.1 km)

Without a car you can spend several days alternating swims, coffees and walks without depending on anyone. A good first afternoon: head down to the harbour, walk round the lake, climb to the higher part to see it from above and finish on a terrace in the centre. You will find more ideas in [what to see in Agios Nikolaos](/blog/que-ver-en-agios-nikolaos/).

## Easy bus trips

KTEL intercity buses connect Agios Nikolaos with many places in the east of the island, including Elounda, Plaka, Kritsa, Ierapetra, Sitia and Heraklion. They are a convenient and inexpensive way to get around on your own. Some ideas for a day out:

- **Kritsa.** In about 20 minutes by bus you reach this mountain village, known for its handmade textiles and embroidery. Shortly before the village is the Byzantine church of Panagia Kera, with frescoes from the 13th and 14th centuries. If you like walking, very close to the village the Kritsa Gorge begins, about 4 km long. We tell you about it in [Kritsa and its surroundings](/blog/kritsa-y-alrededores/).
- **Elounda and Plaka.** Two coastal villages facing Spinalonga, with seafront walks. From Plaka, the boat crossing to the island takes only a few minutes.
- **Ierapetra.** On the south coast, a little under an hour away by bus. It has a long seafront promenade with cafés and the Kales fortress at the entrance to the old harbour, a good excuse to swap seas for a day.

![Solo hiker seen from behind on a stony path through olive groves, with a small white village and the bay below](../../../assets/blog/gen/viajar-solo-a-agios-nikolaos-1.jpg)

Timetables change with the season and the day of the week, so check them on the KTEL website or ask at the station before you set out. Out of season there are fewer services: always check what time the last bus back leaves. More tricks in [getting around without a car](/blog/moverse-sin-coche/).

## By boat around Mirabello Bay

Boat trips leave the harbour for Spinalonga and other spots around the bay, some with a stop for a swim. Travelling solo, they are a simple way to spend the day in company without having to organise anything. Spinalonga was a Venetian fortress and, between 1903 and 1957, a leper colony; it is an impressive visit. The island has very little shade, so bring water, a hat and comfortable shoes. Another option is to take the bus to Elounda or Plaka and cross from there.

## Eating alone in a taverna

In Crete, eating alone is the most normal thing in the world. Tavernas are informal places, nobody bats an eyelid at a table for one and, at the ones by the water, the view keeps you good company. A few ideas:

- **Order several small dishes** instead of one big one: a salad, some vegetables, a local cheese. That way you try more things.
- **Ask what is on that day.** Many tavernas prepare seasonal dishes that do not always appear on the menu.
- **Adjust the amount.** Portions can be generous; if you are not very hungry, say so when you order.
- **Do not be surprised** if at the end they bring you a little raki or a dessert on the house: it is a common custom in many tavernas on the island.

![Small table for one on a seaside terrace set with a Greek salad, fried vegetables, cheese and bread, facing the water](../../../assets/blog/gen/viajar-solo-a-agios-nikolaos-2.jpg)

A mid-morning coffee in a *kafeneio*, the traditional Greek coffee house, is also a good way to watch neighbourhood life go by. You will find more tips in [how to choose a taverna](/blog/como-elegir-una-taberna/).

## Meeting people along the way

Travelling solo does not mean being alone all the time. If you fancy some company now and then:

- **Guided walking tours.** The town offers guided walks, some with a tasting of *mezedes*, which you can book online or through local agencies.
- **Group excursions.** Boat trips or day trips to places such as Knossos or the Lasithi Plateau bring together travellers from many countries, and conversation comes naturally.
- **Guided hiking.** If you want to follow mountain trails and would rather not go on your own, there are guides who organise walks in the area.

## A room of your own

At the pension we have a single room with panoramic views, designed for people travelling solo. From the pension's balconies you can watch the sun rise over Mirabello Bay, and there is no better way to start the day than with a coffee or a tea in front of that landscape, before deciding what you feel like doing.

## Practical tips

- **Let someone know your plans.** If you are going walking in the mountains or through a gorge, tell someone your route and the time you expect to be back.
- **Avoid the heat.** In summer, do the long walks early and save the middle hours of the day for the sea or for resting.
- **Carry plenty of water**, on boat trips too.
- **Keep the contact details handy.** Save the pension's phone number (+30 2841 14 15 17) in your phone in case you need any directions.
- **Carry some cash.** It can come in handy in small places or in villages.

Agios Nikolaos is a good destination for travelling solo precisely because it does not ask much of you: you can spend the day between the sea and the centre without a car, and go on an excursion whenever you feel like it. Check bus and boat timetables the day before and leave some free gaps in your plan; they are often the best moments of the trip.

Most people who come to Agios Nikolaos land at Nikos Kazantzakis airport in Heraklion, on the eastern edge of the city. From there to Agios Nikolaos it is about 62 km, roughly 50 minutes by car if there is no traffic. There are three main ways to make the journey: rental car, taxi or bus. We explain how each one works and what to bear in mind.

## By rental car

This is the most practical option if you plan to get around eastern Crete. At the airport there are many rental companies, local and international, with a desk or a pick-up point. In high season it is wise to book in advance, and to check carefully what the insurance covers before you sign.

### The route

The way is simple: on leaving the airport, take the national road along the north of Crete (the VOAK, also signposted as E75) heading east, following the signs for Agios Nikolaos. The road passes Hersonissos and Malia, then heads inland through the Neapoli area and finally drops down towards Mirabello Bay and Agios Nikolaos.

![Two-lane highway with a wide paved shoulder beside the sea, dry hills and olive trees on one side and blue water on the other](../../../assets/blog/gen/como-llegar-desde-el-aeropuerto-de-heraclion-2.jpg)

A few useful things about this road:

- **There are roadworks.** Several stretches between Hersonissos, Neapoli and Agios Nikolaos are being upgraded, and there may be diversions, narrowed lanes or occasional closures. It is worth checking the local news or your navigation app before setting out.
- **The hard shoulder is used for letting others pass.** It is customary for slower vehicles to pull over onto the hard shoulder to make overtaking easier. Do not feel obliged to do so if you are not sure, and overtake only with good visibility.
- **At night, take it easy.** After a long flight, the last part of the journey, with its bends, is more tiring. If you arrive worn out, consider taking a taxi that first night.

### Arriving in Agios Nikolaos

Head for the harbour, where the statue of the abduction of Europa stands and there is a large free public car park. From there, the pension is about 3 minutes on foot (200 m). You can see the map and the distances on [our location](/#location).

If you want to make the most of the car afterwards, we have ideas for [half-day driving routes](/blog/rutas-en-coche-de-medio-dia/) from Agios Nikolaos.

## By taxi

This is the most comfortable way, especially if you arrive at night, are travelling with children or have a lot of luggage. At the exit of the arrivals area there is a taxi rank, and you can also book a transfer in advance with a company of your choice.

Prices change with the season and the time of day, so ask the driver for an approximate price before setting off, or request a quote in writing if you book online. To give the destination, just say: 24 Sarolidi Street, Mylos area, Agios Nikolaos, next to Kitroplatia beach.

## By bus (KTEL)

Crete's green intercity buses are run by the KTEL company. For this journey, the one that interests us is **KTEL Heraklion–Lasithi**, which links Heraklion with Agios Nikolaos.

![Green intercity coach at a bus stop with its luggage hold open and travellers with suitcases waiting to board](../../../assets/blog/gen/como-llegar-desde-el-aeropuerto-de-heraclion-3.jpg)

### Where they leave from

Buses to Agios Nikolaos leave from Heraklion's main bus station, near the port. Many of those heading east also have a stop on the main road, a short walk from the airport terminal, although which services stop there can vary with the season and the roadworks. Before travelling, look up the timetables from the airport stop on the KTEL Heraklion–Lasithi website.

If no bus suits you from that stop, the alternative is to take the blue city bus from the airport to the centre of Heraklion and, from the main station, the bus to Agios Nikolaos.

### What is worth knowing

- **It is slower than the car**, because it stops in several towns along the way.
- **Timetables change** between summer and winter, and out of season there are fewer departures. Always check the KTEL website before you go, and do not leave the last bus of the day as your only option.
- **Large luggage** goes in the bus's luggage hold.
- **The Agios Nikolaos station** is at the entrance to the town coming from Heraklion, some distance from the harbour. With suitcases, the easiest thing is to take a short taxi ride to the pension.

If you are going to spend a few days without a car, our guide to [getting around without a car](/blog/moverse-sin-coche/) in the area may be useful.

## What about Sitia airport?

East of Agios Nikolaos, near Sitia, there is a small airport with few flights, mostly domestic. It is about 70 km away by road, over an hour of bends, and does not have good bus connections. For most travellers, Heraklion is more practical; Sitia is only worth looking at if you find a flight that suits you well.

It is also worth knowing that a new international airport is being built at Kastelli, southeast of Heraklion. Until it opens, everything we say here refers to the current airport.

## Summary for arrival day

- **Rental car:** the most flexible option if you are going on excursions. About 50 minutes without traffic.
- **Taxi:** the most comfortable, especially at night or with children.
- **KTEL bus:** the cheapest, but slower and with timetables that change with the season.

And if your flight lands late, plan the journey in good time: at those hours the options narrow, and it is better to have it sorted before take-off.

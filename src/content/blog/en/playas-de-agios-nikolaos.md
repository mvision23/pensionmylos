In Agios Nikolaos you can go for a swim without taking the car: there are four beaches less than half an hour's walk from the pension and several more a short drive away. None of them is the best for everyone. It depends on whether you're with children, whether pebbles bother you, whether you want shade or whether you'd like somewhere to eat next door. This is how we would choose.

## Three Questions Before Choosing

- **Sand or pebbles?** Ámmos, Ammoudi, Almiros, Voulisma and Elounda beach are sandy. Kitroplatia and Havania mix sand and fine shingle. Plaka is pebbly.
- **Do you need shallow water?** If you're with small children, Ámmos, Ammoudi and Almiros slope in gently and are usually calm.
- **Facilities or peace and quiet?** Almost all the beaches near the town are organised, with sunbeds, umbrellas and a bar. If you're after something without facilities, you have to go a little farther.

## The Town's Beaches, on Foot

### Kitroplatia (1 minute, 100 m)

The one right next door. It's a small beach of sand and fine shingle, in front of a square with large tamarisk trees that give shade and with tavernas and cafés all around. It has umbrellas and showers, and the water is usually calm. The name comes from the citron, a citrus fruit that was traded here in the last century.

It's perfect for a quick swim in the morning or between visits. In high summer it fills up, so it's not the choice if you want room to spread out your towel.

### Ámmos (10 minutes, 650 m)

A sandy beach next to the marina, with shallow water that is normally calm, which makes it very comfortable for small children. There are sunbeds, umbrellas and showers, and plenty of places for a drink nearby. Being in the middle of town, it's also usually fairly busy.

### Ammoudi (18 minutes, 1.2 km)

North of the centre, in a large, well-sheltered bay, protected from strong northerly winds. It's a small sandy beach with shallow water and tamarisks that give some natural shade. Locals and visitors alike like it, so on some days it fills up.

### Almiros (26 minutes, 2.1 km)

To the south, the town's broadest sandy beach. The water is shallow and usually calm, ideal for children. It lies at the mouth of the Almiros river, which carries water all year round, and for that reason the sea is quite a bit colder in some spots.

![Wide sandy beach with calm shallow water and, behind it, reeds, eucalyptus and tamarisks where a stream meets the sea](../../../assets/blog/gen/playas-de-agios-nikolaos-2.jpg)

Behind it is a protected wetland, with reeds, eucalyptus and tamarisks, where you can see birds, especially in spring and autumn, when migrating species stop over. On the beach there are sunbeds, umbrellas, water sports and a beach bar. It's the town's most popular beach, so in August it's best to go early.

## A Short Drive Away

### Havania

On the outskirts of town, on the road to Elounda. It's a beach of sand and soft shingle, with very clear water and rocks at the ends that are worth a look with a snorkelling mask, because there are usually small fish. It's an organised municipal beach; water shoes are handy for the rocks.

### Voulisma, Next to Istro

About 12 km away, next to the village of Istro, it's one of the best-known sandy beaches in the area: pale sand, turquoise and shallow water. It's organised, with sunbeds and bars, and gets very crowded in July and August. If you go in high season, it's better first thing in the morning or at the end of the afternoon.

### Towards Elounda and Plaka

Elounda is about 23 minutes away by car (15 km). Its main beach is sandy, with calm, shallow water, and is organised. A little farther on, in Plaka, the beach is pebbly, with tamarisks and views of Spinalonga.

![Pebble beach with clear turquoise water and tamarisks, and a low rocky island with old stone walls on the horizon](../../../assets/blog/gen/playas-de-agios-nikolaos-3.jpg)

If you're after somewhere without facilities, on the peninsula opposite Elounda is Kolokytha, an unorganised cove reached by a dirt track and a bit of a walk. There's hardly any shade, so bring everything you need. There's more about the area in [Elounda and Plaka](/blog/elounda-y-plaka/).

## Choosing According to What You're After

- **With small children:** Ámmos, Ammoudi and Almiros, for the sand and shallow water. You'll find more ideas in [Agios Nikolaos with children](/blog/agios-nikolaos-con-ninos/).
- **Natural shade:** Kitroplatia and Ammoudi have tamarisks. At the rest, a rented or your own umbrella.
- **Without a car:** the four in town. Almiros is a bit farther, but it's an easy walk.
- **To see the seabed:** Havania and rocky areas in general. Bring a mask and snorkel.
- **A long sandy beach:** Almiros or Voulisma.
- **Peace and quiet:** Kolokytha, with no facilities, or any of the town's beaches first thing in the morning.
- **Eating next to the water:** Kitroplatia and Ámmos, with tavernas and cafés a few steps away.

## The Wind and the Blue Flag

In summer the meltemi often blows, a northerly wind that in Crete comes from the northwest and is usually stronger in the afternoon. Mirabello Bay gives quite a lot of shelter, and beaches such as Ammoudi are protected from the north wind, but on windy days the sea gets choppy in the more exposed spots. If wind is forecast, choose a sheltered beach and check the forecast before heading out.

As for the Blue Flag, the municipality of Agios Nikolaos, which also includes Elounda, is among those with the most awarded beaches in Greece. The list changes every year, so if it matters to you, check the current year's on the programme's official website in Greece (blueflag.gr).

## To Finish

If it's your first day, start with Kitroplatia or Ámmos, which are a step away, and leave Almiros, Havania or Voulisma for when you know what kind of beach you like.

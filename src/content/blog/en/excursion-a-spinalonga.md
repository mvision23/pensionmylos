Spinalonga is one of the trips we get asked about most, and it's easy to organise on your own. The islet lies at the entrance to the Gulf of Elounda, north of Agios Nikolaos, and in a single morning there's time to cross, explore it unhurriedly and be back for lunch. Here are the ways to get there, what you'll find and what's worth bringing.

## An Islet with Two Histories

Spinalonga, also called Kalydon, is small: about eight and a half hectares. It shouldn't be confused with the peninsula of the same name that closes off Elounda Bay. The island is the walled rock you see opposite the village of Plaka, about 750 metres from the coast.

### The Venetian Fortress

The Venetians laid the first stone of the fortress in 1579, on the remains of an ancient acropolis, to control the entrance to the gulf. It was so solid that it stayed in their hands after the rest of Crete passed to the Ottomans in 1669, and they did not hand it over until 1715. Afterwards a Muslim population settled there, which in the 19th century grew into a village of several dozen families. As you walk around the island you'll see traces of both the Venetian and the Ottoman periods.

![Weathered stone ramparts with wild grass in the cracks, overlooking a blue gulf and distant hills](../../../assets/blog/gen/excursion-a-spinalonga-2.jpg)

### The Leper Colony (1903–1957)

In 1903 it was decided to turn the island into a leper colony, and the first patients arrived in 1904. For more than half a century they lived isolated there, with their houses, shops, church and hospital. The colony closed in 1957, when an effective treatment already existed, and the last residents were transferred to a facility in Attica. It was one of the last active leper colonies in Europe. According to the sources, the last inhabitant, a priest, left in 1962.

Many people arrive with this history already in mind thanks to Victoria Hislop's novel *The Island* and the Greek series based on it, *To Nisi*.

## How to Get to Spinalonga

There are three main options. All three work well; the difference is how much time you want to spend on the boat.

### From Plaka: The Shortest Crossing

Plaka is the village with Spinalonga right in front of it, and the boats cross in about ten minutes. It's the best option if you're driving and want to go at your own pace: you arrive, cross, and on the way back you can swim or eat in the village.

### From Elounda: More Departures

Regular boats leave the harbour of Elounda for the island, quite frequently in season. The trip takes about 15–20 minutes. There's more going on and more services than in Plaka, and some boats add a loop around the bay.

From the pension, Elounda and the Spinalonga area are about 23 minutes away by car (about 15 km). There are also scheduled buses (KTEL) from the Agios Nikolaos station to Elounda and Plaka. Their timetables change with the season, so check them on the company's website or at the station itself. If you're not going to rent a car, you'll find more details in [how to get around without a car](/blog/moverse-sin-coche/).

### From the Harbour of Agios Nikolaos: An Organised Excursion

Boat excursions to Spinalonga leave from the harbour of Agios Nikolaos, about 3 minutes' walk from the pension. The crossing is longer, about 45–60 minutes each way, but you see Mirabello Bay from the water. Many include a swimming stop, often in the Kolokitha area, and some include lunch on board.

Before choosing, look carefully at what each one includes: total duration, stops, whether there's a guide and whether the entrance to the site is included. Prices and timetables change every season; the most practical thing is to ask at the stalls in the harbour or compare online.

## The Entrance Fee and Time on the Island

If you go on the scheduled boats, the entrance to the archaeological site is paid separately, at the ticket office on the island, and in high season there may be a queue. Some organised excursions include it and others don't.

![Narrow stone-paved lane between old weathered stone houses, with a few visitors walking away in the distance](../../../assets/blog/gen/excursion-a-spinalonga-3.jpg)

Allow an hour and a half or two hours to see it without rushing. The usual route:

- You enter through **Dante's Gate**, a passage about 20 metres long through which the patients arrived.
- You continue along the **main street**, with the houses and shops from the leper colony period, several of them restored.
- You climb up to the **Venetian bastions**, with views over the Gulf of Elounda and Plaka.
- You can complete the **path around the island** beside the walls, about 1.5 km.

We recommend reading up a little before you go. It's a place of remembrance, and the visit is understood much better when you know who lived there and in what conditions.

## Practical Tips

- **Season.** The boats run mostly from spring to autumn. In winter departures are greatly reduced and the site may have shorter hours or close, so if you're coming out of season, confirm beforehand. In [when to visit Agios Nikolaos](/blog/cuando-visitar-agios-nikolaos/) we explain how the area changes through the year.
- **Heat and shade.** There is very little shade on the island and in summer the midday sun is fierce. It's best to go first thing or at the end of the afternoon.
- **Water and protection.** Bring plenty of water, a cap or hat and sunscreen.
- **Footwear.** The ground is uneven stone, with steps and loose gravel. Comfortable trainers are better than flip-flops.
- **Reduced mobility.** The outer path is easier going; the inside of the village has slopes and steps.
- **Swimming.** If you want to swim afterwards, bring a swimsuit and water shoes: in Plaka and on the Kolokitha peninsula there are places to do so. More ideas in [what to bring to the beach in Crete](/blog/que-llevar-a-la-playa-en-creta/).

## How to Fit It into the Day

Spinalonga goes very well with Elounda and Plaka. Early in the morning, the island. Then, a walk along the Elounda isthmus, with its salt pans, its windmills and the submerged remains of ancient Olous. And for lunch, either of the two villages. We cover it in more detail in [Elounda and Plaka](/blog/elounda-y-plaka/).

If you set out early, you'll be back in Agios Nikolaos by mid-afternoon, with time for a swim at Kitroplatia, a minute from the pension. Before you go, confirm the boat and site timetables, because they change with the season.

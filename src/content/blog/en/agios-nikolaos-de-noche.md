When the heat eases, Agios Nikolaos comes out into the street. In summer, the middle hours of the day call for shade and rest, and the town really comes alive from sunset on: families strolling, terraces full and boats rocking beside the quay. Dinner is late here, often after 9 pm.

The good thing is that everything is close. From the pension you can spend the whole evening on foot, with no car or taxis, and walk back afterwards.

## The Evening Stroll

In Greece, the unhurried walk as evening falls has a name of its own: the *vólta*. Here it's almost always done along the water. This is a simple loop, with walking times from the pension:

1. **Kitroplatia (1 minute).** Start in the small square by the beach, with its tavernas right on the sand. At dusk, the bay keeps changing colour.
2. **The headland and the harbour (3 minutes).** Follow the coast to the harbour, where the statue of the Rape of Europa stands out against the sky. It's a good moment to photograph it, in the last light.
3. **The canal and the lake (6 minutes).** Cross the bridge and enter Lake Voulismeni just as the terrace lights come on and are reflected in the water. If you'd like to know something of its history before you go, we tell it in [our article on Lake Voulismeni](/blog/lago-voulismeni/).
4. **The centre (9 minutes).** The streets climbing up from the lake have shops and cafés, and in summer it's common to find businesses open well into the night.

![Families strolling along a cobbled seaside promenade at dusk, with street lamps, tamarisks and a calm blue bay](../../../assets/blog/gen/agios-nikolaos-de-noche-1.jpg)

If you'd prefer something quieter, the cobbled path from Kitroplatia towards the marina and Ámmos beach is less busy and has good views over the bay.

## The Light at the End of the Day

Agios Nikolaos faces east, towards Mirabello Bay. That's why, on full-moon nights, the moon rises above the bay and leaves a long reflection on the water. Before that, the end of the afternoon tints the sea and the mountains on the other side of the bay. We tell you how to enjoy that moment in [our article on the sunset in Agios Nikolaos](/blog/atardecer-en-agios-nikolaos/).

## Dining Late and Unhurried

Dinner is the heart of the evening. Around the lake, in the harbour and in the Kitroplatia area there are many restaurants and tavernas with outdoor tables, and in summer it's normal to eat from nine or ten o'clock.

![Taverna table outdoors at night with shared small dishes, bread and a small carafe of raki with two tiny glasses](../../../assets/blog/gen/agios-nikolaos-de-noche-2.jpg)

A few things worth knowing:

- **The tables by the water are the most sought after.** In July and August, if you want the front row, go early or ask whether they take reservations.
- **There's no rush.** In Greece it's normal to linger at the table, and the bill doesn't usually arrive until you ask for it.
- **Share dishes.** Ordering several starters for the middle of the table is the most common way to dine in a Cretan taverna, and the best way to try a bit of everything.
- **At the end, there's sometimes a treat.** In many tavernas in Crete it's the custom to offer something sweet or a small glass of raki when you finish.

To pick a place without falling for the most touristy ones, you'll find pointers in [how to choose a taverna](/blog/como-elegir-una-taberna/).

## A Drink by the Water

After dinner, the atmosphere gathers around the lake, the harbour and the nearby streets of the centre: bars with terraces, background music and cafés that stretch the evening out. In July and August everything is livelier and people stay up later; in spring and autumn the evenings are quieter, ideal for a drink without the noise.

In winter the town stays alive, because it is the capital of Lasithi and people live here all year round, but some venues geared to tourism close until the following season.

## Special Nights

Some dates completely change the night-time atmosphere:

- **Easter night.** At midnight on Orthodox Holy Saturday, after the announcement of the Resurrection, an effigy of Judas is burned on a platform in the middle of the lake, amid fireworks and firecrackers. A good part of the town gathers on the shores, so arrive in good time. The date changes every year.
- **Nautical Week.** It's usually held in summer, with activities at sea and in the harbour. Its dates vary from year to year, so check the announcements from the town hall.
- **Concerts and summer events.** During the season, concerts and other open-air events are organised. The programme changes every year; ask in town or look it up online when you arrive.

![Fireworks bursting over calm dark water at night, reflected in the surface, with crowds in silhouette along the shore](../../../assets/blog/gen/agios-nikolaos-de-noche-3.jpg)

## Practical Tips for the Evening

- **A light jacket.** In spring and autumn it gets cool when the sun goes down, and by the sea the wind can blow.
- **Comfortable shoes.** The town is built on hills and there are slopes and steps between the centre and the coast.
- **Walk back.** It's about 6 minutes from the lake to the pension, and about 9 from the centre.
- **Patience in high season.** In August the most central terraces fill up; sometimes it's enough to move a street away from the water to find a table.

The evening here doesn't call for complicated plans: a stroll by the water, a long dinner and a relaxed walk home are enough to understand the town. If you'd like to get your bearings before you go out, our [map with distances](/#location) shows everything within walking distance of the pension.
